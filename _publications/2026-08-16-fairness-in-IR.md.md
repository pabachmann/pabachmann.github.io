Provider fairness in information retrieval (IR) often demands fair representation of both individual providers and groups of providers in the retrieval output. We formulate axioms for both types, inspired by similar settings in social choice theory, with the aim of long-term (amortised) fairness across queries.
For individual fairness, we draw on notions of envy-freeness and proportionality from work on fair division. Intersectional group fairness can be expressed as a multidimensional apportionment problem. In contrast to classical social choice settings, we introduce relevance as a key parameter for these axioms.
While these two types of fairness are in general incompatible, we discuss weakened axioms and simplifying assumptions in order to obtain simple model settings where the two can be unified.
